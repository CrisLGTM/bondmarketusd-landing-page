What are fractionalized bonds?+

A corporate bond is a way for a company to borrow money from investors. Fractionalization represents bonds in smaller, tradeable token units, so you can start with a smaller amount instead of buying a whole bond.

On Bond Swap, you trade bond tokens, not company shares. The rights attached to each token are set out in the product’s terms.

Does 24/7 include weekends?+

Yes. You can buy and sell on Bond Swap around the clock, including weekends. Your schedule doesn’t have to follow traditional market hours.

Trading availability doesn’t guarantee an order will fill at your chosen price.

What do I need, and how small can I start?+

Bond Swap uses USDC, and supports trades below $1. A small trade amount is different from the total cost of funding and trading: applicable network, trading, and borrowing costs should be considered separately.

Do bonds pay interest?+

A bond’s stated interest payment is called its coupon. For eligible bond holdings, coupon payments are distributed proportionally, according to the underlying bond and product terms.

Coupons follow the issuer’s payment schedule, not the platform’s 24/7 trading hours. Payments depend on the issuer meeting its obligations.

Can I go long, go short, or lend?+

Yes. You can trade without leverage, or take leveraged long and short positions. Going long means taking a view that the price will rise; going short means taking a view that it will fall.

You can also lend supported assets. Lending is separate from simply holding a coupon-paying bond; its rates and conditions depend on the lending product.

What should I know about the risks?+

Bond prices can fall, issuers can default, and you can lose money. Fractional sizing makes smaller trades possible; it does not remove investment risk.

Leverage magnifies gains and losses and adds borrowing costs and liquidation risk. Lending also carries risk; returns are not guaranteed.
